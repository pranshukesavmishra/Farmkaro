# Engagement brief — revenue advocate, Jabalpur

> A brief for engaging a practising revenue advocate in Jabalpur to convert the

**Engagement scope:** Madhya Pradesh law as applied in Jabalpur district, for leases
concluded through a private platform between a landowner (lessor) and a cultivator
(lessee). Advise as at the date of opinion; flag anything awaiting notification.

---

## Questions

**Q1 — The MP land-leasing enactment.** Our research (docs/02, §flagged) records that MP
enacted a separate land-leasing law based on the Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act 2016.
Confirm: the exact title and year of the enactment; whether it and its rules are **in force
and operational in Jabalpur today**; and whether leases brokered by a private platform fall
within it without any licence or registration requirement on the platform itself.
*Why it matters: our lease templates, and the product's core promise that leases create no
tenancy/occupancy rights and possession reverts on expiry, are built on this framework.*

**Q2 — No-tenancy protection in practice.** Under the framework identified in Q1, confirm
that a written fixed-term lease creates **no occupancy/tenancy right and no adverse-
possession pathway** for the lessee, and what formalities (if any) that protection depends
on — registration, a specific clause, a maximum term, or notification of the revenue office.
*Why it matters: this single assurance is the reason landowners list at all.*

**Q3 — Stamp duty and registration.** For an agricultural lease in MP: the current stamp
duty (rate and base — annual rent vs total rent vs premium), the registration requirement
by term (the Registration Act s.17 threshold as applied in MP), who customarily bears the
cost, and the exact e-stamping/registration procedure at the Jabalpur SRO, including
whether MPIGR's SAMPADA portal handles it end to end.
*Why it matters: our product shows a registration ladder (unregistered → stamped →
submitted → registered) and must describe the real process and cost, not a guess.*

**Q4 — Rent benchmarks.** Our working figure of ~₹15,000/acre/year average rent is flagged
as varying enormously. From the advocate's practice: the realistic rent bands for irrigated
vs unirrigated land in Jabalpur tehsils, and any statutory or customary constraints on rent
or on rent escalation clauses.
*Why it matters: our comparables engine refuses to invent estimates; a practitioner's band
is a legitimate cross-check on what completed leases will show.*

**Q5 — Co-owned (joint khata) land.** For a parcel with recorded co-owners: can one
co-owner lease their undivided share; what consent/signature set makes a lease of the whole
parcel safe; and what verification of shares should be insisted on beyond the khatauni.
*Why it matters: field onboarding (see `field-executive-onboarding.md`) must know when to
require every signature.*

**Q6 — FRA 2006 patta land.** For land held under a Forest Rights Act individual patta
(non-transferable): is any leasing or crop-sharing arrangement lawful at all, on what
conditions, and with whose approval (Gram Sabha?). If the answer is no, we will refuse
such listings outright.
*Why it matters: one of our pilot-district records is FRA patta land; the product currently
holds such parcels for legal check.*

**Q7 — Mortgaged land.** Where the khatauni records a bank mortgage (e.g. KCC): may the
owner lease without the lender's consent; does the lease survive enforcement; and what
disclosure to the lessee is legally required vs merely prudent.
*Why it matters: we disclose recorded encumbrances prominently; we need the rule, not just
the instinct.*

**Q8 — Platform-status questions.** Does anything in MP law treat a leasing platform as an
intermediary requiring registration (e.g. under rent-court/RERA-like regimes, money-lending,
or agent licensing)? And confirm our reading that taking **no pooled investment and holding
no funds** keeps us clear of SEBI CIS territory (docs/02 covers the Growpital precedent).

**Q9 — DPDP consent adequacy.** Review our consent flow for land-record lookups (statement
shown verbatim, checkbox, audit entry storing the statement + user + timestamp; no name
search; single-record-by-own-identifier only). Is this adequate as a DPDP Act 2023 consent
record for the purpose stated, and what would make it stronger?

**Q10 — The lease template.** Review our draft lease instrument [FILL: attach when drafted —
or commission its drafting under this engagement] against Q1–Q3, including the no-tenancy
recitals, termination, and dispute-resolution clauses appropriate for Jabalpur.
